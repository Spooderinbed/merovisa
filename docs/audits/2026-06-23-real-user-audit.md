# MyVisa — Real-user audit (2026-06-23)

**Method:** 7 parallel dimension auditors run as a multi-agent workflow — anonymous + signed-in persona walkthroughs, UX/IA, trust & honesty/provenance, accessibility, security & data boundaries, engineering & performance, design fidelity. Every finding was adversarially re-checked against the real code by a second agent before inclusion, then synthesized and de-duplicated. 17 agents total.

**Audit lens:** an anxious, budget-constrained, non-native-English Nepali student on a low-end mobile, already burned by consultancy hype.

**Role framing** adapted from `msitarzewski/agency-agents`: testing-reality-checker, design-persona-walkthrough, design-ux-researcher/ux-architect, support-legal-compliance-checker, testing-accessibility-auditor, security-appsec-engineer + testing-api-tester, design-brand-guardian.

**Tally:** 48 confirmed findings — 1 P0 · 14 P1 · 21 P2 · 12 P3.

> AI-generated, code-grounded findings (file:line cited). Treat P0/P1 as high-confidence but verify before large changes.

## Verdict

No — a real, anxious Nepali student would not trust this through to the end today, and the failures cluster exactly where trust is hardest-won. The single worst problem is that anonymous results live only in client state with no recoverable URL (P0): a student on a low-end Android that kills background tabs completes all 9 steps, sees their verdict, then loses everything to a refresh, a back-tap, or an app-switch — while the UI promises "your assessment expires in 3 days," a promise the product structurally cannot keep for anyone who doesn't finish Google sign-in in one unbroken session. Compounding that, the conversion gate — the most trust-sensitive moment in the whole flow — dangles a fabricated "23-step guide" and "14 documents" that don't exist, then resolves post-signup to "coming soon, we'll email you" when there is no email path anywhere in the codebase: this is the precise consultancy bait-and-switch the product was built to oppose, shipped by the product itself. The honesty engine is also leaky at the edges: the lowest verdict band ("reach") tells a near-ineligible 3/100 student they just have "a few key areas" to strengthen, the matches page shows a profile-less signed-in user fabricated "Reach" verdicts with "Grade short by 60%" off fields they never filled, and an AccuracyMeter brands every honest user "25% · Basic" with raw percentages the product swore never to show. What genuinely works deserves credit: the scoring engine is server-side, rule-based and versioned; the DHA financial cap is honestly sourced; source/verified attribution lines exist; and the dashboard already does the right empty-profile gating that matches and results fail to copy — so most fixes are about propagating discipline the codebase already demonstrates, not inventing it. But until persistence, the fabricated gate, and the verdict-honesty leaks are fixed, the product is quietly dishonest in exactly the ways its positioning promises it is not.

## Findings by severity

### P0

**Anonymous results live only in client state — refresh, back, or a killed tab wipes the entire assessment, and the promised "3-day" recovery is unreachable.**
User impact: A student on a low-end Android (which aggressively kills backgrounded tabs) on a flaky connection finishes the 9-step wizard, sees their verdict, then accidentally backs out, pulls to refresh, or switches apps — and is dumped back at step 1 with everything gone. The assessment IS persisted in the DB for 3 days, but the student is never given a URL to it, so the only path back is completing Google OAuth in the same uninterrupted session. The UI literally says "your assessment expires in 3 days," a promise an anonymous user has no way to act on.
Evidence: `components/assess/assess-flow.tsx:15-19` holds profile/payload/assessmentId in `useState` and renders `<Results>` inline (never navigates to a durable URL); `components/wizard/use-wizard-state.ts:50-52` keeps all wizard input in `useState` with zero persistence; `app/(focused)/assessment/[id]/page.tsx:15` redirects any non-authenticated visitor to `/assess`, so the persisted id (`app/api/assess/route.ts:120-126`) is never viewable anonymously.
Fix: After a successful anonymous assess, route to `/assessment/[id]` and allow anonymous read of a non-expired, unclaimed assessment by id, so refresh/back/return-within-3-days all work. At minimum, persist wizard state and payload to `sessionStorage` so a refresh mid-flow or mid-results doesn't reset to step 1.

### P1

**Conversion gate fabricates a "23-step guide" and "14 documents" that don't exist, then resolves to a "coming soon, we'll email you" promise the product cannot keep.**
User impact: At the highest-stakes trust moment — anonymous-to-account conversion — the app blur-teases two concrete deliverables, neither real, then on the post-signup owned view shows bare titles plus "Coming soon — we're preparing this and will email you when it's ready." No 23-step guide exists; the "14 documents" count is hardcoded while the real checklist is dynamic (~20 items, never a fixed 14); and there is no email-delivery path anywhere in the repo. The sibling `ConversionPaths` even states in code that the only way to keep an assessment is a Google account — directly contradicting the teaser's email promise. This is the exact consultancy bait-and-switch the product exists to defeat.
Evidence: `components/results/gated-teasers.tsx:4-13` hardcodes the "23-step" and "14 documents" TEASERS; line 27 renders the unlocked state with "...will email you when it's ready." `components/results/results.tsx:88` renders `GatedTeasers` `unlocked={owned}`. `lib/checklist/generator.ts:114-345` generates a variable item set. Flagged and still unfixed in `docs/audits/2026-06-14-mvp-promise-behavior-audit.md:107`; `tests/components/results/gated-teasers.test.tsx:8-9` locks the fabricated strings into the suite.
Fix: Remove the non-existent guide and the hardcoded count. Tease only what genuinely unlocks (the real dynamic checklist and plan, both built), drop the "we'll email you" line, and update `gated-teasers.test.tsx` so the suite stops enforcing the lie.

**On a persist failure, every "Continue with Google" / unlock CTA silently disables under live urgency copy — the student cannot save or sign up, with no explanation or retry.**
User impact: On the infra-degraded path the API deliberately tolerates (anonymous persist returns `id:null` but still 200s with the full payload), the student sees their verdict and matches plus "Your assessment expires in 3 days … Create a free account with Google to keep it" — but every CTA is greyed out and unclickable, with no message and no retry. The conversion goal is dead while the urgency copy still tells them to act.
Evidence: `app/api/assess/route.ts:119-131` returns `id:null` on a persist miss by design (console-logged only); `lib/auth/start-claim-oauth.ts:14-15` returns early on null id; all three CTAs disable on null id under active urgency copy — `components/results/conversion-paths.tsx:24` (`disabled={!assessmentId}`, copy at 18-22), `components/results/conversion-prompt.tsx:24`, `components/results/university-matches.tsx:131,139`. No fallback or retry exists.
Fix: On null assessmentId, don't pair active urgency copy with a dead button. Either fall back to sign-in-then-rescore (sign in, re-POST the in-memory profile), or render an honest inline message ("We couldn't save this — sign in and we'll re-run it") with a working action.

**Signed-in matches page fabricates "Reach" verdicts for an empty profile — "Grade short by 60% (needs 60%)" off fields the user never filled.**
User impact: A returning student who signs in directly (via `/auth` or the AppBar, not through the wizard) has no profile row. `/matches` — a top-level nav item on desktop and mobile — renders with no empty-profile guard; `computeMatches` coerces every missing input to 0, so `gradeGap = 60-0 = 60` forces `verdict='reach'` for essentially every program, and ProgramCard prints "· Grade short by 60% (needs 60%)" and "· IELTS overall short by 6.5 (needs 6.5)" verbatim. The header even asserts "We compare your Nepal TU percentage directly against each program's minimum" when there is no percentage. The student's absence of data is presented as a score of zero, telling them every Australian program is out of reach.
Evidence: `app/(app)/matches/page.tsx:33-66` runs `computeMatches` with no completeness gate (the only empty state needs `matches.length===0`, which never fires on the full catalogue); contrast the dashboard's gate at `app/(app)/dashboard/page.tsx:31`. `lib/matches/compute.ts:65-68` defaults nulls to 0; `:84` forces reach on `gradeGap>10`; `:102-118` emit the verbatim reasons. Empty-profile sign-in is reachable: `app/auth/callback/route.ts:43-44` redirects to `/dashboard` with no profile created when no claim token is present.
Fix: Mirror the dashboard's profile-incomplete handling on `/matches` — when `academic.gradePercent` and `english.overall` are both null, render an onboarding empty state with a CTA to `/profile` or `/assess` instead of zero-defaulted scoring. Alternatively, have `computeMatches` return a distinct "insufficient-data" verdict so the UI never prints "short by 60%" off an unentered field.

**Lowest verdict band ("reach") tells a near-ineligible student they have "a few key areas" to strengthen — copy never scales with severity.**
User impact: The product's core promise is an honest verdict, kindest where it matters most: the weak student. But the floor band is "reach," whose static headline reads "This is ambitious — focus on strengthening a few key areas." A 40% higher-secondary student with no IELTS and ~USD 7k budget scores academic=3, financial=29, weighted=36 — still "reach." Telling a student with a 3/100 academic fit they have just "a few key areas" materially understates how far below the line they are — the precise harm (doomed applications) the platform claims to prevent.
Evidence: `lib/scoring/verdict.ts:9-20` — `mapVerdict` only ever returns strong|possible|reach (any dim < `minDimensionFloor=30` → reach). `components/results/verdict-card.tsx:15-19` — reach copy is a fixed string. Persona confirmed by running `lib/scoring/engine.ts` on [grade=40, higher-secondary, not-taken English, 1M NPR, scholarship-dependent] → verdict=reach, academic=3. Thresholds at `lib/data/policy/verdict-thresholds.ts:31`.
Fix: Add a distinct lowest band (e.g. "Unlikely as-is") when a core dimension is critically low, or make the reach copy conditional on severity and name the blocking dimension plainly ("Your grade is well below what these programs expect") instead of the blanket "a few key areas." Kind ≠ falsely hopeful.

**AccuracyMeter shows every honest, wizard-complete user "Profile accuracy: 25% · Basic" — a demoralizing raw percentage the product swore never to show, with higher levels mathematically unreachable.**
User impact: A student who answers all 9 questions truthfully is told their "Profile accuracy" is 25% (28% with an English test) on a near-empty bar. For an anxious user this reads as "my assessment is only 25% accurate," which is false — the number reflects only whether documents were uploaded, and no built flow ever raises it. It renders on every results page, anonymous and owned, and shows a raw 0-100 percentage to users — a direct violation of the never-show-raw-scores rule.
Evidence: `lib/results/accuracy.ts:16` hardcodes `completeness = 25`; only `+3` when English `taken` (max 28); line 28 thresholds (`>=40 ? "Verified"`) are unreachable. `components/results/accuracy-meter.tsx:9` renders `{completeness}% · {level}` as visible text; `:15` sets the bar width. `lib/results/assemble.ts:49` wires it into every payload; `results.tsx:89` renders it unconditionally.
Fix: Stop showing a raw percentage here. Reframe as an honest qualitative line ("Self-reported — upload documents later to keep them on file") with no percent and no near-empty bar, and remove the dead "Verified"/"Complete" levels.

**API failure on /assess instructs "refresh" — which destroys all 9 steps of input, and there is no Retry.**
User impact: When scoring fails (DB hiccup, 429 rate-limit, network blip — all common on Nepali mobile data), the user sees "Something went wrong … Please refresh and try again." Because wizard state is in-memory only, refreshing resets to step 1. The app's own instruction causes the data loss, and there is no Retry that re-POSTs the completed profile. On slow connections this is also reachable as a hang: the `/api/assess` fetch has no timeout/abort, so a slow request leaves the student on a pulsing "Analyzing your profile" indefinitely.
Evidence: `components/assess/assess-flow.tsx:60-65` renders "Please refresh and try again." with no button; `handleComplete` (27-46) retains the profile but has no retry handler; the fetch (34-39) has no AbortController. The 429 path (`app/api/assess/route.ts:24-29`, 10/min, 100/day per IP) is easily hit on shared/NAT Nepali IPs.
Fix: Replace "refresh" with a "Try again" button that re-calls `handleComplete(profile)` from retained state; distinguish 429 (wait) from 500; add a fetch timeout with a visible "slow connection / still working" state. Never instruct a refresh while unsaved wizard state exists.

**Document upload rejects PDFs — but transcripts, offer letters, loan sanctions, bank statements and CoEs are PDF-native for real students.**
User impact: The documents vault advertises kinds (Bachelor's Transcript, University Offer Letter, Education Loan Sanction Letter, CoE, OSHC, Sponsor Income Tax Return) that Nepali students receive as PDFs, not photos. Uploading the actual PDF returns "File must be JPG, PNG, or WebP," forcing the student to screenshot or photograph a PDF (degrading exactly the documents that must stay legible) or simply give up. This breaks a core Phase-5 surface for its most common real inputs.
Evidence: `app/api/documents/upload/route.ts:18` `ALLOWED_TYPES = ['image/jpeg','image/png','image/webp']`, rejected at 48-50; `components/documents/document-card.tsx:116` `accept="image/jpeg,image/png,image/webp"`; `verifyImageMagic` (route line 74) enforces image magic bytes. Kinds in `lib/documents/types.ts:3-7` are overwhelmingly PDF in practice.
Fix: Accept `application/pdf` with a PDF magic-byte check and a PDF-aware viewer/thumbnail; at minimum reword the kinds/copy so expectations are set. Image-only is the wrong constraint for a visa-document vault.

**Plan actions and the shortlist "Applied" button fail silently — and shortlist can deadlock its own disabled state permanently.**
User impact: On a flaky connection, tapping Done/Dismiss/Mark-in-progress on a plan item, or Shortlisted/Applied on a program, does nothing visible when the request fails — the card just doesn't change, so the user taps repeatedly and loses trust. Worse, the shortlist button has no `.catch()`, so a thrown network error skips `setBusy(false)` and leaves all three status buttons permanently disabled until a full reload. "Applied" is the product's core outcome-capture trigger, so a silent failure here also drops the data moat.
Evidence: `components/plan/plan-item-card.tsx:23-32` swallows the failure and no caller surfaces an error. `components/matches/shortlist-button.tsx:26-36` has no try/catch and no `.catch`; on rejection both `setStatus(next)` and `setBusy(false)` (line 35-36) are skipped, leaving `disabled={busy}` stuck true. The correct pattern already exists at `components/profile/editors/section-save.tsx:40-49`.
Fix: Mirror the SaveFeedback pattern — show an inline "Couldn't save — try again" and always reset `busy` in a `finally`. Add `.catch` to shortlist-button so a network error never permanently disables the control.

**No error boundaries anywhere in app/ — an uncaught server error shows the raw Next.js error page, breaking the "calm authority" trust premise.**
User impact: Any uncaught error (a Supabase auth/network throw in a server component, a malformed payload, a render error on `/assessment/[id]`, `/matches`, or `/dashboard`) drops an anxious student onto the default unbranded Next.js error screen. For a product whose entire value is calm, trustworthy authority, a jarring stack-trace-style failure on a slow mobile destroys trust at the worst moment.
Evidence: No `error.tsx`/`global-error.tsx`/`not-found.tsx`/`loading.tsx` exists anywhere in `app/`, and no React error-boundary symbol exists. `app/layout.tsx:25-34` wraps children only with `<AnalyticsProvider/>`. `app/(app)/dashboard/page.tsx:47` awaits a 6-way `Promise.all` of live Supabase queries with no fallback; `app/(app)/matches/page.tsx:33` a 4-way.
Fix: Add `app/global-error.tsx` and at least one `app/error.tsx` (or per-group in `(app)`/`(focused)`) rendering a calm, branded "something went wrong — try again" card with a `reset()` button. Small, high-leverage, directly on-thesis.

**Keyboard focus is invisible on the budget slider and every text input (WCAG 2.4.7) — and the trust-critical "verified · source" line plus the "Possible" verdict both fail colour contrast (WCAG 1.4.3).**
User impact: A keyboard-only user, or anyone whose touch fails on a cheap phone, cannot see which control is focused on the budget slider and every auth/profile text input — focus is invisible or a near-imperceptible 1px border shift. Separately, the faint-grey `verified <date> · <host>` attribution line (3.29:1) — the platform's core honesty signal — and the "Possible" verdict badge (#b07d22, 3.04:1 on its own tint) — a core banded-verdict signal — are both too low-contrast to read reliably for low-vision users or on a glary low-end screen in daylight. The text meant to prove honesty, and the verdict that IS the product, are among the hardest to read.
Evidence: `app/globals.css` defines no `:focus-visible` rule; `components/ui/slider.tsx:28` applies `outline-none` with no ring restored; inputs use `outline-none focus:border-primary` (e.g. `components/auth/auth-card.tsx:77`). Contrast computed: `--ink-faint #8a877d` = 3.29:1 on `--bg` (used in `components/results/source-line.tsx:21`); `--possible #b07d22` = 3.04:1 as text on `bg-possible-tint` (used `components/results/verdict-card.tsx:35`). Strong/Reach pass; only Possible fails.
Fix: Add one `@layer base` rule — `:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--primary); outline-offset: 2px; }` (plus a forced-colors variant) — and remove the bare `outline-none` from the slider/inputs. Darken `--ink-faint` (light) to ~#6f6c63 and the "Possible" text token to ~#8a5e12 to clear 4.5:1.

**Wizard step changes and the recap→results hand-off give screen readers no announcement and no focus move.**
User impact: A blind student pressing "Continue" hears nothing change — focus stays on the button while a new question silently swaps in. The "Analyzing your profile" recap auto-advances to results with no spoken cue, then results render with focus left wherever it was and no `<h1>` to land on. The single most important moment — the verdict appearing — is silent and invisible to a screen-reader user.
Evidence: `components/wizard/wizard.tsx:68` swaps the step via `<div key={w.stepKey}>` with no aria-live, no focus move, no role; `components/assess/profile-recap.tsx:51-60` fires `onDone()` via `setTimeout` with the heading a plain `<span>`; `components/results/results.tsx` has no `<h1>` and no focus target (top heading is the VerdictCard `<h2>`).
Fix: On step change, move focus to the step `<h1>` (`tabIndex={-1}` + `ref.focus()` keyed on stepKey). Give ProfileRecap `role="status" aria-live="polite"`. On results mount, focus the verdict heading and promote it to the page `<h1>`.

### P2

**Profile recap mislabels PTE/TOEFL scores as "IELTS" (e.g. shows "IELTS 65.0" for a PTE 65).**
User impact: During the "Analyzing your profile" interstitial — the exact moment a student checks that we understood them — a PTE/TOEFL taker sees their score relabeled IELTS with a forced one-decimal ("IELTS 65.0"). For an anxious non-native speaker this looks like the tool got their test wrong, right before the verdict.
Evidence: `components/assess/profile-recap.tsx:20-25` hardcodes `` IELTS ${p.englishScore.toFixed(1)} `` regardless of `p.englishTest`; the wizard stores `englishTest` and already has per-test formatting (`components/wizard/steps/english-step.tsx:67`).
Fix: Label the recap line from `p.englishTest` ("PTE 65", "TOEFL 79", "IELTS 7.0") and only apply `toFixed(1)` for IELTS, reusing the existing helper.

**Education grade is percentage-only via a slider in the anonymous wizard — Nepali students with CGPA/GPA cannot enter their real grade.**
User impact: The wizard forces a 40–100 percentage slider, but many TU/KU bachelor's report CGPA on 4.0 and NEB +2 students hold a GPA. In the conversion-critical first impression, these students must mentally convert GPA→percentage with no guidance — exactly the error that produces a wrong verdict. The signed-in profile editor already offers a grade-system select with CGPA normalization, so the gap is specifically the anonymous wizard.
Evidence: `components/assess/.../education-step.tsx:20-46` offers only a percentage Slider; the wizard hardcodes `gradeSystem:'percentage-nepal'` (`use-wizard-state.ts:30`). The richer path exists: `components/profile/editors/academic-editor.tsx:63-70` + `lib/matches/from-student-profile.ts:38` (`normalizeGradeToPercentage`).
Fix: Add a grade-system toggle (percentage / CGPA-4 / GPA) in the wizard with on-the-fly conversion, reusing `normalizeGradeToPercentage`.

**Auth's "Other ways to sign in" reveals a fully-styled email form that does nothing.**
User impact: A privacy-wary student who distrusts Google opens "Other ways to sign in," fills the required email, taps "Create account & save" — and gets only "Email sign-in is coming soon." A form that looks fully functional (required field, submit button) is a placeholder — a bait-and-switch on the one page asking them to commit.
Evidence: `components/auth/auth-card.tsx:23-26` `submitEmail` just sets a "coming soon" notice; the real-looking form is at 66-88.
Fix: Either build email sign-in or replace the interactive form with a plain "Email sign-in is coming soon — use Google for now" line, so there's no false affordance on the conversion-critical auth page.

**No loading.tsx on async server pages — blank/frozen screen while multiple Supabase round-trips resolve on slow connections.**
User impact: Navigating to `/dashboard`, `/matches`, or `/assessment/[id]` on slow Nepal connectivity shows nothing — no spinner, no skeleton — until all server data resolves. With no streamed loading state the page feels frozen, and the user backs out or double-taps.
Evidence: No `loading.tsx` and no `<Suspense>`/`React.lazy` anywhere; `app/(app)/dashboard/page.tsx:47` (6-way + conditional 2-way `Promise.all`), `app/(app)/matches/page.tsx:33` (4-way) block the whole route.
Fix: Add `loading.tsx` paper-card skeletons matching the design tokens for the `(app)` routes so the App Router streams an instant shell.

**"Guide" occupies a primary nav slot on every signed-in screen but is a "Coming soon" dead-end, while the fully-built per-program Checklist appears in no nav at all.**
User impact: On mobile there are only 5 tabs, and one whole slot goes to Guide, which always lands on "coming soon" — recurring disappointment for a student hunting for help. Meanwhile `/checklist` is a real, working surface reachable only by deep-link from a program card or the plan, so users who don't stumble onto it never know it exists. Prime navigation is spent on a non-feature while a built feature is undiscoverable.
Evidence: `components/layout/mobile-tab-bar.tsx:8-14` and `components/layout/app-bar.tsx:17-24` list Guide; `app/(app)/guide/page.tsx` is a pure stub. Grep for `/checklist` finds links only in `components/matches/program-card.tsx` and the checklist components — never in the nav.
Fix: Demote Guide out of the primary tab set until Phase 6 ships and surface Checklist in the nav/tab bar (or a persistent dashboard entry) so the built feature is reachable.

**Document preview dialog has the right role but does not move, trap, or restore focus.**
User impact: When the document preview opens, keyboard/SR focus stays behind the overlay; the user can Tab through the obscured page underneath, and on close focus is not returned to the View button. Disorienting for keyboard and SR users (gated behind a successful upload, so P2).
Evidence: `components/documents/document-viewer-modal.tsx` sets `role="dialog" aria-modal="true"` and handles Escape + scroll-lock (14-31) but never focuses into the dialog, has no focus trap, and restores no trigger reference; wired from `components/documents/document-card.tsx:145-151`.
Fix: On open, focus the Close button and trap Tab within the dialog; on close, restore focus to the opener. A small `useFocusTrap` hook also covers future checklist/guide modals.

**No prefers-reduced-motion support despite pervasive entrance, stagger, and pulse animations; matches tabs expose no tablist ARIA and convey selection by colour alone; the budget slider announces the raw integer.**
User impact: Users with vestibular disorders get rising/fading content on nearly every screen with no off switch (the recap stacks five staggered slide-ups plus a pulsing dot at the high-stakes "analyzing" moment). On `/matches`, a screen-reader user hears three independent buttons with no indication one is selected or that they share a panel (selection is colour-only). A blind student setting their budget hears "4500000" with no currency.
Evidence: `app/globals.css` has no `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)`; `profile-recap.tsx:64-68` staggers via inline delay + `animate-pulse`. `components/matches/matches-tabs.tsx:10-37` renders plain `<button data-active>` with no `role="tab"/tablist/tabpanel`/`aria-selected`. `components/ui/slider.tsx` sets `aria-label` but no `aria-valuetext`.
Fix: Add a reduced-motion `@layer base` block; apply the standard tabs ARIA pattern (`tablist`/`tab`+`aria-selected`+`aria-controls`/`tabpanel`); thread a `valueText` prop into Slider and set `aria-valuetext` to the formatted currency.

**/api/leads accepts any email against any assessment GUID with no ownership proof (lead injection); rate limiting fails open when Upstash is unset; per-dimension 0-100 scores and the weighted total ship to the client.**
User impact: An unauthenticated attacker can write junk emails tied to assessment ids into the leads table with no auth (harm currently limited — ids are random UUIDs and no email-sending code exists). The IP-keyed limits on the two unauthenticated endpoints (`/api/assess`, `/api/leads`) silently disable when Upstash env is unset or the limiter throws, so the very first prod deploy starts with limits OFF unless the env is present day one. And the raw 0-100 dimension scores plus weighted total ride along in the assessment payload to the browser — one DevTools Network tab away — undercutting the "never numbers to users" promise (the scoring rules/weights themselves stay server-side, so this is exposure, not a rule leak).
Evidence: `app/api/leads/route.ts` validates only `{email, assessmentId}` with no auth and is orphaned at runtime (no client calls it). `lib/rate-limit/upstash.ts` returns `true` (allowed) when the limiter is null and on catch (allow-on-error). `lib/scoring/types.ts:136-159` exposes `weighted` and `DimensionScore.value`, embedded in the payload returned from `app/api/assess/route.ts` and passed to the client `results.tsx`; `factor-bars.tsx` uses `dim.value` for bar width.
Fix: Delete the orphaned `/api/leads` route (the live funnel already records the lead after an HMAC-verified claim) or gate it with `verifyClaim`. In production, treat missing Upstash config as a hard fail for the unauthenticated endpoints and fail closed on limiter exceptions. Strip `weighted` and per-dimension `value` from the payload (compute the bar fraction server-side or coarsen to band tiers), or document the decision explicitly.

**User menu dropdown uses `shadow-sm`, violating the absolute "no shadows ever" rule; the checklist-landing CTA hand-rolls `text-white` + `hover:opacity-90` instead of the design-system Button (a real dark-mode contrast regression).**
User impact: The account dropdown — visible to every signed-in user on every page — renders a drop shadow, reading as a generic SaaS dropdown and quietly eroding the restrained, flat, trustworthy feel that is the product's differentiator. Separately, the "Browse matches" CTA uses `text-white` instead of the `--on-primary` token, which in dark mode (where `--on-primary` is the dark #08231f meant to sit on the lighter teal) flips the intended contrast into a harder-to-read label.
Evidence: `components/layout/user-pill.tsx:30` `... bg-surface shadow-sm` — the only shadow occurrence in the tree; spec line 404 "No shadows ever." `components/checklist/checklist-landing.tsx:29` `bg-primary ... text-white hover:opacity-90` — the only default-palette colour class in the tree; canonical button is `bg-primary text-on-primary hover:bg-primary-ink` (`components/ui/button.tsx:13`).
Fix: Drop `shadow-sm` (the existing `border border-line` already separates the surface). Replace the hand-rolled anchor with `<Button>`, or at minimum swap `text-white`→`text-on-primary` and `hover:opacity-90`→`hover:bg-primary-ink`.

### P3
